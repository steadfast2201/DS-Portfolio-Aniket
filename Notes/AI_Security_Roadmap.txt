AI SECURITY ROADMAP AND INSTITUTE PROGRAMS
Prepared October 24, 2026 for a data scientist (about 3 years of experience) moving into AI security and building a product.
Fees, dates and intakes change, so confirm each item on its official page.

==================================================
TWO MEANINGS OF "AI SECURITY"
==================================================
When you browse courses, you will see two different things under this label:

1. Security OF AI: protecting models, data and LLM apps from attacks. This is your target.
2. AI FOR security: using AI to catch hackers, spot phishing and automate security teams. Useful background, but a different job.

Most formal programs, especially degrees, mainly teach the second with a little of the first. Check the syllabus for terms like "adversarial ML", "LLM security", "red teaming" or "model risk" before paying for anything.

==================================================
PLAIN-LANGUAGE GLOSSARY
==================================================
- Security: stopping bad actors from breaking, stealing or misusing things. For AI, that means tricking a chatbot, stealing a model or poisoning its training data.
- AI governance: the rules and accountability around AI. Who approves a system, how you prove it is safe, fair and legal, and who is responsible when it goes wrong.
- Prompt injection: hiding instructions in text to trick an LLM into ignoring its rules.
- Data poisoning: sneaking bad data into training so the model behaves wrongly.
- Model theft: copying a model by querying it many times.
- Data leakage: a model revealing private training data.
- Adversarial examples: tiny changes to an input that fool a model.
- Red teaming: deliberately attacking your own system to find weaknesses before real attackers do.

==================================================
YOUR ADVANTAGE
==================================================
You already know how models are trained, evaluated and deployed. Most security people do not, and most governance people (lawyers, risk managers) do not either. The main gap to fill is general security knowledge.

==================================================
THE ROADMAP (ABOUT 12 MONTHS, PART-TIME)
==================================================

PHASE 1 (months 1-3): Security basics from zero
Learn how security works before adding "AI" to it.
- Key ideas: the CIA triad (keeping data secret, unchanged and available), threats vs. vulnerabilities vs. risks, how attackers think, networks, authentication, encryption, cloud basics.
- Courses: Google Cybersecurity Certificate (Coursera); TryHackMe "Pre-Security" and "Intro to Cyber Security" paths; Professor Messer's free Security+ videos on YouTube.
- Optional certificate: CompTIA Security+, widely recognised and a useful base under AI credentials.

PHASE 2 (months 3-7): Core AI security
How AI systems get attacked and defended.
- Free references: OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, MITRE ATLAS (a catalogue of real attacks on AI), NIST's adversarial machine learning taxonomy.
- Hands-on: Gandalf by Lakera (a prompt-injection game), TryHackMe and Hack The Box AI/LLM rooms, and open-source red-teaming tools such as Microsoft PyRIT and NVIDIA garak.
- Topics: prompt injection, jailbreaks, data poisoning, model theft, data leakage, adversarial examples, and risks from AI agents that can take actions.
- Certifications to consider: Certified AI Security Professional (CAISP) and similar. SANS/GIAC reportedly planned four AI-focused certifications by the end of 2026, so check what is available now.

PHASE 3 (months 6-10): Depth in red teaming and defenses
- Attack and defend a real LLM app or RAG pipeline.
- Study how to secure agents and tool use.
- Publish write-ups of what you find. In a new field, credibility comes from being visibly useful.

PHASE 4 (any time): Light governance
Customers will ask about compliance, so know the basics without making it your focus.
- NIST AI Risk Management Framework (free).
- A skim of the EU AI Act, which sorts AI systems by risk level.
- ISO/IEC 42001, the international standard for an AI management system that companies can be audited against.
- If selling in India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act.
- Certifications to revisit later: IAPP AIGP (no prerequisites; the current body of knowledge took effect February 2, 2026), ISO 42001 Lead Implementer (roughly US $2,000-4,300 depending on provider), and ISACA's AAISM for AI security management.

PHASE 5 (months 8-12): Find a problem and build
Do not wait until you finish learning. Start talking to people around month 6.
1. Interview 20+ security leaders, compliance managers and ML team leads. Ask: "What is painful about proving your AI is safe?"
2. Pick one narrow problem. Examples: an inventory of every AI tool and model a company uses; automated red-teaming of LLM apps before launch; real-time guardrails against prompt injection and data leaks; evidence collection for audits; vendor risk checks for third-party AI tools; monitoring models for misuse.
3. Study existing players (for example Protect AI, HiddenLayer, Lakera, Credo AI, Holistic AI) to see what is covered and where the gaps are.
4. Build a small working version (MVP) and run it free with 3-5 design partners.
5. Later, expect larger customers to ask vendors for SOC 2 or ISO 27001.

Your data science skills fit automated testing, monitoring and evidence generation well. Pure policy-document tools are crowded and easier to copy.

INSTITUTE AND UNIVERSITY PROGRAMS
==================================================

Short, part-time, India-friendly
- IIT Roorkee x Futurense, PG Certificate in AI/GenAI-Powered Cybersecurity: a 9-month hybrid program (the first batch began October 18, 2025) covering threats like deepfakes, AI-generated phishing and LLM vulnerabilities. Check whether a 2026 batch is open.
- IITM Pravartak x Simplilearn, "Smart Shield" AI-Powered Cybersecurity program: aimed at lower to mid-level professionals, with 70+ hands-on labs across offensive and defensive security, AI-powered cybersecurity and compliance. A gentle start, but leans toward "AI for security".
- IIT Kanpur C3iHub x NFSU Bhopal: a 5-day hybrid program on Agentic AI for Cybersecurity, open to industry professionals. The June edition has passed, so watch for repeats.
- MeitY Electronics & ICT Academies (IITs and MNIT): short, low-cost online programs on AI-enabled cybersecurity open to working professionals.

Full degree: part-time or online (abroad)
- Purdue (USA), online: MS in Cybersecurity and Trusted Systems with an applied AI and cybersecurity pathway covering machine learning and LLM risks, plus a 9-credit graduate certificate in applied AI and cybersecurity. The certificate is a low-risk way to test the waters.
- FIU (USA), online: MS in Computer Engineering: Security with Cybersecurity and AI tracks, 30 credits over six eight-week terms.
- University of San Diego (USA): MS in Cyber Security Engineering, available online, part-time on campus or full-time on campus.
- Griffith University (Australia), online: Master of Cyber Security and Artificial Intelligence, 100% online, 1.5 years full-time or part-time equivalent.
- UPenn (USA), online: MSE in Software Systems & Cybersecurity, with full-time or part-time schedules. Strong on fundamentals but not AI-specific.

Full-time, in person
- University of San Diego: new full-time on-campus MS in Cyber Security Engineering (36 units over four semesters; first intake September 2026, so the next realistic intake is 2027).
- IIIT Sri City: has offered MTech programs in AI/ML and cyber security. Current details were not verified, so check directly. IIT and IIIT security MTechs are worth exploring for a full-time Indian degree.

==================================================
RECOMMENDATION
==================================================
With 3 years of experience and a product goal, avoid a full degree at first:

1. Months 1-3: free or low-cost fundamentals (Google Cybersecurity and TryHackMe).
2. Months 3-9: one structured program for accountability, either the IIT Roorkee PG Certificate (if open) or the Purdue graduate certificate, alongside OWASP and ATLAS material and your own projects.
3. Decide on a master's later, once you know whether research, a job or your product is the main path.

A degree makes the most sense if you need a visa, want a research career or your employer will sponsor it. For a product, projects and customer conversations carry more weight.

==================================================
ONGOING HABITS
==================================================
- Follow OWASP, MITRE ATLAS and NIST updates; the field moves fast.
- Build small public projects, such as a script that tests an LLM for prompt injection.
- Join communities like OWASP AI security chapters and AI security groups on LinkedIn and Discord.
